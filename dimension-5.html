    Section Dimension 5: Inclusion through cultural diversity

    This dimension focuses on how to honor culture (starting with students’ and mentors’ cultural funds of knowledge) and use it to build an equitable community of mentors and mentees. Curriculum and mentors’ practices are designed to facilitate cultural exchanges and celebrate diversity. Implicit biases and manifestations of inequities, marginalization, (lack of) privilege or representation, and cultural appropriation are exposed and openly discussed.
    🔗
    There are two components to exposing students to cultural diversity, one being affirming a student’s cultural identity and the other being showing them a broader range of cultures. The first point is especially important when working with under-represented populations. Often, activities are implicitly presented with examples from the dominant culture which can make them less useful to these students and can make them feel isolated. The answer is to intentionally craft activities that allow students to feel validated in their cultural identity and allows them to share their culture with others.
    🔗
    Similarly, we want to expose students to other cultures so they can in turn appreciate other cultures, while reaffirming their identities, and finding common grounds and connections. This includes mathematical practices that give a different approach to doing math. However, you do need to take care to learn enough about the cultures and at a deep enough level to avoid cultural appropriation, or falling in stereotypes. Even details such as the pronunciation of different words is important, as well as presenting original sources of knowledge.
    🔗
    In terms of mathematics teaching and learning, culture and decolonization also plays a role: it means not seeing the student as someone with deficits that needs to be tutored to fix a gap, but rather as a complex human being with lots of mathematical potential to be realized, through high expectations. It also means that mentors are also learning from the students.
    🔗

    • I believe students learn more when their cultures and real-life experiences are valued.

    • I believe a safe and inclusive classroom allows all students to share openly and confidently.
      🔗
    • I believe cultural diversity enriches learning by offering multiple ways to see things.
      🔗
    • I believe students deserve role models who reflect their identities and inspire them.
      🔗
    • I believe learning is deeper when it connects to music, stories, traditions, and knowledge from many cultures.
      🔗
    • I believe families and communities are valuable in building culturally responsive learning environments.
      🔗
    • I believe respectful conversations about differences can help students grow as thoughtful and caring citizens.
      🔗
    • I believe I must reflect on my own biases to model openness and fairness.
      🔗
    • I believe embracing cultural diversity is key to equity, joy, and meaningful learning.

    Subsection Component 1: Honor cultural identities

    Value who students are and where they come from, showing pride in their cultures and making their practices visible.
    🔗

    Mentors’ Actions.

    • Learn about, acknowledge, and celebrate each student’s cultural identity.
      🔗
    • Ensure that students and their cultures feel valued and represented in the activities.
      🔗
    • Share parts of your own culture, including beliefs and personal stories.
      🔗
    • Highlight positive cultural role models.
      🔗
    • Celebrate cultural events and traditions as opportunities for learning together.
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Component 2: Tie content with culture

    Intentionally connect teaching content to cultural experiences, world scenarios, and diverse perspectives.
    🔗

    Mentors’ Actions.

    • Talk about students’ real-life contexts and current or relevant events, highlighting cultural aspects.
      🔗
    • (Planning) Have students learn about literature, religion, media, music, art, math & science, technology, and other knowledge from diverse cultures as a natural part of the teaching material.
      🔗
    • (Planning) Acknowledge historical contributions of diverse groups in the content that you are exploring.
      🔗
    • Include student choice in parts of the activities, encouraging cultural expression and voice.
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Component 3: Implement inclusive practices

    Design learning spaces and activities where all voices, traditions, and cultural ways of learning feel welcome.
    🔗

    Mentors’ Actions.

    • Create a safe space for learning and sharing that validates all cultures.
      🔗
    • Incorporate cultural ways of interacting in your teaching (with student consent, avoiding appropriation).
      🔗
    • Launch prompts or collaborative tasks where students can learn from one another’s cultural backgrounds.
      🔗
    • Encourage students to bring cultural artifacts, stories, or relevant examples from home.
      🔗
    • (Planning) Invite families or community members, beyond the usual learning space, to share cultural knowledge or experiences.
      🔗
    • Use multilingual resources (including visual languages) or allow students to use their home languages when possible.
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Component 4: Promote respect and critical awareness

    Guide students beyond their comfort zones to explore unfamiliar cultures, discuss differences respectfully, and challenge stereotypes.
    🔗

    Mentors’ Actions.

    • Invite students to explore and discover aspects of cultures that they are less familiar with.
      🔗
    • Teach students to discuss cultural differences and opinions respectfully.
      🔗
    • Recognize and address stereotypes or biases when they arise, modeling critical thinking.
      🔗
    • Show how cultural perspectives can shape different approaches to the ideas explored.
      🔗
    • Reflect on your own assumptions and biases, and model openness in revising them.
      🔗
    🔗
